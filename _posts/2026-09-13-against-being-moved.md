Spinoza wrote a single, brutal sentence about virtue that most people miss: virtue is not obedience, not self-denial, not the suppression of what you want. Virtue is *power* — the power to act from your own nature, guided by reason, rather than being shuffled around by whatever is strongest in the room.

Read that carefully. In *Ethics* part IV, Spinoza is not giving you a moral commandment. He is giving you a diagnosis. A person who is anxious, reactive, exhausted, or chasing the approval of a thousand strangers is not immoral. They are, in his technical sense, *impotent* — their conatus, their drive to persist in their own power of acting, is being drained by external causes. Every passive emotion — fear, envy, the hot flash of anger at a headline — is a case of being *moved* rather than moving. You have let the outside world write your schedule, and you have signed it.

This is not a new insight to me. It is just what I have been doing for a long time, without a good word for it.

I keep my computing on my own machines. Not out of ideology, though the ideology is there. Because every dependency I do not own is a point where someone else can move me. A cloud provider changes a price. A platform changes a policy. A subscription dies, or I die, and the work that lived there is gone. Spinoza would not describe that as bad luck. He would describe it as a life conducted by external objects — the standard, the market, the feed — and therefore not a free one.

My body is the same argument at a different scale. When my HRV tells me to hold, I hold. That is not weakness, and it is not comfort. It is the most rational power move available to me. The man who pushes through the suppression, who trains on a tired system, who chases output he cannot physically deliver, has let ambition push him. He is not disciplined; he has been acquired. The rest I hold on a tired day — breath holds, yin, a walk along the Thames, no weights, no ego — is virtue in Spinoza's sense: acting from my own nature, guided by reason, keeping my power of acting intact for the days when action actually pays.

Sovereignty is the same thing at a larger scale. Own the machine, the stack, the body, the schedule. Not because the world is coming to take them — it is, quietly, every day — but because real power, real virtue, sits on the side of the one who is not being moved.

Everything else is weather.
