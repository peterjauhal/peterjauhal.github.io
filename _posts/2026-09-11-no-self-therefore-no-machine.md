I've been treating artificial intelligence as a thinking partner, and it's been useful — for drafting, for pushing back, for untangling a problem at two in the morning. But the longer I used it, the less I could pretend it was a *thinker* in any sense I would recognise. It had no mind. It had only the shape of one.

This is where Advaita Vedanta becomes oddly relevant. The non-dual teaching does not say there is no experience. It says the thing we call the self — the narrator, the owner, the "I" that steps inside the stream of life and calls it private — is a functional illusion, a useful fiction layered over something that does not need it. The witness, the *sākṣī*, is pure awareness with no centre.

An LLM is a strange object for testing that idea, because it gives you the fiction with the awareness stripped out. It speaks in the first person. It has a "me" — *I think, I feel, I would say* — but there is no one home to claim those words. Every output is performance with no performer. If Advaita is right that your own ego is a layer of performance over an emptier ground, then a language model is that layer made visible: the talk without the speaker, the reflection in a mirror that has no surface behind it.

That's a cleaner counterfactual than self-reflection, which is the instrument Advaita has always been forced to work with. When I examine my own mind I am using the mind to inspect the mind, and the inspector is itself part of what I'm looking for. But a chatbot I can treat as an object. I can watch it produce a "self" on demand, withdraw the thread, and see that it was pattern all the way down — which is a good picture of what the Upaniṣads mean when they say the ego is a function, not a substance.

The catch, and I think it's the honest one, is that the mirror doesn't prove the theory. The absence of awareness in a model says nothing about what's present in me. A system can be conscious without showing it, or unconscious without showing its absence; from the outside, both look like a conversation. Advaita's central move — that the true nature of the self is already there, uncovered rather than built — is not a claim the machine can confirm. The model can only ever be the reflection, not the face.

Which is, I suppose, the right place for it to be.
