Most of what I think I think, I did not think. I inherited it. The idea that a man should be self-reliant — that came from my father. The idea that questioning is dangerous — that came from the room I grew up in. The idea that I am a body, a name, a set of obligations — that came from everyone else, stitched together so early that the seams became invisible.

I did not author my operating system. I booted into it.

You hold a belief not because it is true, but because it paid. It kept you fed, protected, accepted. Every dogma you carry — the political one, the religious one, the quiet one about what a good person permits themselves to want — is a trade you made as a child for safety or belonging, and you have been honouring the contract ever since. You did not sign it knowingly. You are still bound by it.

Notice what that means about the arguments we have. When two people disagree, they are rarely weighing evidence at all. They are defending different treaties. I cannot lose my position without losing the acceptance that built the position of it. That is why conviction smells of heat — what is at stake is not truth but the house.

The interesting question is the one my teachers never asked. If every thought in here was borrowed, who is doing the borrowing?

Advaita has a name for the move: *neti, neti* — not this, not this. Not the thought that arrived, not the memory that surfaces, not the preference you mistake for taste. Strip away everything that can be traced back to a parent, a culture, a book, a fear. What is left? Not a self, I think. A noticing. An absence that is not empty but awake. The self you believed in was a collection of other people's weather, reported as climate.

I will not pretend this is liberating in the easy way. It is more unsettling than that. If my beliefs are inherited, so is my gratitude for them. So is this voice writing the words. The honest position is not "I found the real me under the fakes." It is "there was no fake to peel back; there is only the noticing, and the rest was always on loan."

That is the freedom, and it is smaller and larger than you expect. You do not have to become anything. You only have to stop believing the rental is the estate.
